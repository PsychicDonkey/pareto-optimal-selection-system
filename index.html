d is the majority mean minus the minority mean in within-group SD units. Correlations are within-group. Edit the lower triangle; the upper triangle mirrors it.

    Method and assumptions
    • Predictors and criteria are multivariate normal with the same covariance matrix in both groups; groups differ only in their means. All values are in within-group SD units with the minority mean set to 0.
    • Selection is top-down on the weighted composite with one common cutoff for everyone. Group membership is never used to rank applicants.
    • Expected criterion score is the mean criterion score of those selected, standardised to the whole applicant pool (Appendix A; the pool SD uses the square root that the printed formula omits). The AI ratio is the minority selection rate divided by the majority selection rate.
    • The frontier comes from a grid search over non-negative weights that sum to 1, followed by a non-dominated filter. This approximates the paper's normal-boundary intersection method; small differences reflect grid resolution. The weights chart uses the supported frontier points (the frontier's convex hull), which removes small grid artefacts from the weight paths.
    • In the paper's example the majority group is White applicants and the minority group is Black applicants, using US meta-analytic estimates (Bobko et al., 1999). Replace the parameters with local estimates before using the results for a real decision.

    De Corte, W., Lievens, F., & Sackett, P. R. (2007). Combining predictors to achieve optimal trade-offs between selection quality and adverse impact. Journal of Applied Psychology, 92(5), 1380–1393.
